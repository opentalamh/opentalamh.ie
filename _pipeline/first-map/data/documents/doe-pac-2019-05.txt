Re: Property transfers from religious congregations as contributions towards the costs
incurred by the State in responding to residential institutional child abuse.
Dear Mr Lenihan,
You will recall that I undertook to provide the Committee of Public Accounts with regular updates
in regard to progress being made in completing the transfer of properties offered by religious
congregations as contributions towards the costs incurred by the State in responding to residential
institutional child abuse.
This letter and the accompanying tables summarise developments since my update in late 2018
and notes the current position in relation to the remaining uncompleted property transfers. The
position in regard to cash contributions is also set out.
As the Committee is aware, properties are being transferred by religious congregations under two
distinct rounds: the legally binding 2002 Indemnity Agreement and those offered voluntarily by
congregations in 2009. Considerable progress has been made since my last report, with the transfer
of six further properties being completed. There has also been extensive engagement between all
of the State parties involved and with the transferring congregations and their legal representatives.
Significant milestones have been achieved in the case of a number of the remaining transfers.
The position in regard to the properties offered under the two rounds is summarised below while
Appendix | (2002 Indemnity Agreement) and Appendix 2 (2009 voluntary offers) provide further
details regarding each individual property.
2002 Indemnity Agreement
Following the recent completion of three transfers (Coiscéim and Emohruo, Cappoquin, Co.
Waterford and Waterpark National School, Waterford) only two properties remain to fully transfer
under this round.

Cash contributions
I would also like to advise the Committee of the position in regard to cash contributions offered
by the congregations on a voluntary basis in 2009. A recent contribution of €2 million from the
Congregation of Christian Brothers has brought to €105 million the total amount contributed by
congregations, comprising contributions of some €103.6 million plus added interest of €1.382
million. These amounts have been received by the Department and placed in an investment account
established by the National Treasury Management Agency. This funding is available to Caranua
(the Residential Institutions Statutory Fund). A further €6.4 million in cash contributions is
required to achieve the maximum amount of contributions of €110 million set down in statute that
may be made available to Caranua.
A contribution of €6.8 million, being the balance of the cash contribution of the Congregation of
Christian Brothers, is expected to be received over the coming months. The Congregation have
informed me that these funds will be remitted upon the completion of the sale of lands at Clonkeen
in Dublin: the transaction is currently in process. The amount of that contribution that is in excess
of the statutory cap of €110 million, will go towards the National Children’s Hospital as provided
for in section 43 of the Residential Institutions Statutory Fund Act 2012 as will any additional cash
contributions.
I will continue to provide the Committee with regular updates on these property transfers.
