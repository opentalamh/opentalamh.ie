Re: Contributions from religious congregations towards the costs incurred by the State in
responding to residential institutional child abuse.
Dear Ms Falsey,
I refer to your letter of 1*' October on behalf of the Committee of Public Accounts seeking a further
update from this Department in regard to a number of matters raised at the Committee’s meeting
of 27 September.
As the Committee is aware, properties are being transferred by religious congregations under two
distinct rounds: the legally binding 2002 Indemnity Agreement and those offered voluntarily by
congregations in 2009. While the indicative timeframes previously given to the Committee in
relation to the completion of individual property transfers are not being achieved, it is the case that
substantial progress is being made in relation to the completion of most of the outstanding transfers
and I am confident that a number of these will fully complete over the next few months.
The position in regard to the properties offered under the two rounds is summarised below while
Appendix 1 (2002 Indemnity Agreement) and Appendix 2 (2009 voluntary offers) provide further
details regarding each individual property.
2002 Indemnity Agreement
Five properties remain to fully transfer under this round. In the update I provided to the Committee
in July, I indicated that it was expected that four of these properties would have fully transferred
by the end of Quarter 3 of this year. The current position is that it is likely that three of the
properties will have transferred by the end of the current year and a further property will transfer
in the first half of 2019. The position in regard to the properties concerned is set out in Appendix
1 and may be summarised as follows:

e Coiscéim and Emohruo, Cappoquin, Co. Waterford (Appendix 1 — Properties 3 and 4 —
transferring together)

Closing documents are with the transferring congregation. Once the completed documents
are satisfactorily returned, the properties will then be fully transferred.

e Sacred Heart Centre, Waterford (Appendix 1 — Property 5)

This property has transferred but a first registration is required with the Property
Registration Authority.

e Waterpark National School, Waterford (Appendix 1 — Property 2)

All outstanding issues have been resolved in relation to this property and contracts are
awaited from the transferring congregation. The consent of the Charities Regulatory
Authority will then need to be sought by the transferring congregation.

e Mounthawke secondary school, Tralee, Co. Kerry (Appendix 1 — Property 1)
Confirmation has been sought from the Congregation’s representatives that outstanding
boundary related litigation (not involving the Minister) has been resolved. Once that
confirmation has been received it should be possible to progress the transfer. However it is
not possible to give a definitive timeframe for the completion of the transfer.

It should be noted that in the case of the five properties referred to above, the intended recipient is

in occupation of the property and providing services from it. The material relevance of the transfer

process in these cases is to provide legal ratification of the existing situation where the State is

already benefiting from the use of the properties.

2009 voluntary offers

Of the 18 properties offered voluntarily in 2009 and accepted for transfer to State bodies, 9 have

fully transferred. Considerable progress has been made in relation to the transfer of the remaining

properties. The position in regard to the properties concerned is set out in Appendix 2 and may be

summarised as follows:

e St. Bernard’s Group Home, Fethard, Co. Tipperary (Appendix 2 - Property 10)
In my July update to the Committee it was projected that this property would have fully
transferred by the end of Quarter 3: that target has not been achieved. However, all consents
have been obtained, contracts have been signed and all that remains is that the deed of
transfer and conveyance need to be registered. The transfer should therefore complete
shortly.

e Adult Education Centre, Waterford and land at Cahir, Co. Tipperary (Appendix 2 -
Properties 11 and 12)
Ministerial consents have been obtained in relation to these properties and contracts have
been signed. In the case of the Adult Education Centre, Waterford the transferring
congregation has sought the consent of the Charities Regulatory Authority and it will do so
in the case of the land at Cahir. On receipt of each consent, the transfer can complete. It is
expected that the transfers will complete by end 2018.

e Former school Ballyjamesduff, Co. Cavan, McAuley Centre, Kells, Co. Meath and former
school Westport, Co. Mayo (Appendix 2 - Properties 13, 14 and 17)

These properties have been accepted by the Minister for Education and Skills as required
under statute. The consent of the Minister for Public Expenditure and Reform to accept these
properties was sought on 15" October. If the Minister consents, contracts will then be signed
and it will be a matter for the transferring congregations to seek the consent of the Charities
Regulatory Authority. It is expected that these three transfers will complete by mid-2019.
e The National Rehabilitation Hospital, Din Laoghaire, Blaithin, Gracepark Road, Dublin 9,
and St Anne’s Lenaboy, Galway (Appendix 2 - Properties 15, 16 and 18)
These three properties are taking longer to progress. Particular issues are delaying these
transfers including the complexities of the transfer and the range of stakeholders involved.
In due course it may be necessary to seek ministerial consents and where required the consent
of different entities including State bodies such as the Charities Regulatory Authority.
St Anne’s, Lenaboy Castle, Galway
In your letter you asked for a note for the Committee on a number of issues relating to the transfer
of this property - which is being transferred to Galway City Council by the Western Province of
the Congregation of the Sisters of Mercy. This Department is not directly involved in the transfer
process. From enquiries made to the Council I understand that as part of the agreement between
the two parties it was the Council that requested that the Congregation have a geophysical survey
carried out and therefore the survey was commissioned by the Congregation. I understand also that
the results of the geophysical survey have not yet been provided to the Congregation.
I will continue to provide the Committee with regular updates in regard to these property transfers.
