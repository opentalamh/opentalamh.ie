Item 1: A note on meetings held with Religious Organisations since 2009 to include:
e List of all meetings and minutes/contemporaneous notes taken
e Outcome of each meeting.
In the immediate aftermath of the publication, on 20 May 2009, of the report of the
Commission to Inquire into Child Abuse (the “Ryan Report”) the Government began to
consider the issues arising. A number of meetings with the religious congregations took
place, initially involving the Taoiseach and/or officials from his Department and all of which
involving the Minister for Education and Skills and/or his officials.
The purpose of the initial meetings with the religious congregations was to discuss the
findings of the publication of the Ryan Report and the response thereto, particularly the calls
made by Dail Eireann, in a unanimous motion, that substantial contributions were required
from the congregations by way of reparation. The 15 April 2010 meeting discussed the
voluntary offers made by the congregations in late 2009, the need to augment those offers and
the uses to which the contributions would be put (including the proposed statutory fund for
former residents — now Caranua). In 2010, bilateral meetings were held between
Government officials and representatives of all 18 congregations. In 2011, a meeting took
place between the then Minister for Education and Skills and 14 of the congregations.
Separate meetings were subsequently held with the remaining congregations. Since 2011,
only bilateral meetings have been held.
Details of these meetings are set out in the tabular format below. Where applicable, copies of
minutes or notes of these meetings are enclosed.

Item 2: A note on the service provided by Barnardos to help people reconnect with
families and any issues in relation to this service.
With Department of Education and Skills’ support, Barnardos has provided an Information
and Tracing service, known as the “Origins” service, to former residents of institutions since
2002. Access to the Origins service has been on a self-referral basis. Having previously also
dealt with applications for records from former residents of industrial and reformatory
schools, the service has, since 2007, dealt exclusively with tracing family members on behalf
of former residents of the institutions named in the schedule of the Residential Institutions
Redress Act 2002.
The service’s professional counsellors meet with clients to discuss the process and to explain
any information obtained, etc. The service arranges contacts between a client and any family
members identified subject to necessary consents being obtained and it mediates between the
client and family members as required.
The Service currently operates out of the Barnardos office in Dublin. The operation of the
service is governed by the terms of a Performance Delivery Agreement between Barnardos
and the Department.
About one third of cases now lead to reunion with a family member. Some applicants are
seeking assistance with issues such as locating family graves and understanding complex
family histories. The service is also dealing with reactivated enquiries from previous
applicants who need support with difficult family tracing outcomes. The Barnardos team
remain available to provide practical and emotional support to former residents and their birth
families.
Officials meet regularly with Barnardos to review the service and the latest meeting was held
on 11 April 2017. The congregations are co-operating fully with the service and Barnardos
have confirmed that they regularly request information from the various religious

Additional Note 1: Properties transferring under the 2002 Indemnity Agreement

As the Committee will be aware, the Department agreed in principle with CORI that a total of

64 properties would be accepted subject to good and marketable title and agreed valuations.

This number has reduced to 61 as the Department has accepted and received a cash sum in

lieu of three properties where good and marketable title could not be established.

Since the last update in December 2016, three further properties have been finalised resulting

in a position as at the current date, that a total of 53 properties have been fully transferred
under the terms of the Agreement and there are no outstanding issues. The total value of these

properties is €53,405,975. This represents 84% of the total monetary value of all the

properties transferring under this aspect of the Indemnity Agreement excluding cash in lieu.

A list of these properties and their valuations are attached at Appendix 1.

Work is continuing to complete the legal requirements to finalise the transfer process of the

remaining 8 properties under the terms of the Indemnity Agreement — a list of those

properties is attached at Appendix 2. This Department expects that a number of properties

will be fully transferred under the Agreement in the next few months.

The Department is continuing to actively engage with the Chief State Solicitor’s Office who

liaise with the solicitors for the congregations and the solicitors acting on behalf of the HSE.

Additional Note 2: Christian Brothers’ playing fields

Subsequent to the Committee’s meeting of 13 April last, the Minister met with Brother Edmund

Garvey, Province Leader of the Christian Brothers in Ireland at Brother Garvey’s request.

Brother Garvey indicated that the Christian Brothers have reconsidered the offer made by Minister

Quinn in relation to ownership of the playing fields whereby they would transfer ownership to the

Edmund Rice Schools Trust (ERST) and that, in the event of any proposed transfer of such lands, the

State would be entitled to receive 50% of the proceeds.

Brother Garvey also indicated that the Christian Brothers plan to dispose of some land currently used

by Clonkeen College and the sale of the land will enable the Christian Brothers to complete their

commitment to make cash contributions as part of their response to the Ryan report.

On 8 May last the Minister received a letter from Brother Garvey setting out this proposal. The

Department intends to engage with the Christian Brothers on this matter and will provide a further

update to the Committee in due course.
