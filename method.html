Method

How Open Talamh finds, checks and grades land held by Catholic Church entities in Ireland, and the data sources it uses.

Open Talamh is building the first open, parcel-level atlas of land held by Catholic Church entities in Ireland. Every parcel traces back to a primary record, and every parcel carries a confidence grade. The method is being piloted in County Louth before it is extended to other counties and, later, to State bodies.

Principles

  • Every claim links to a folio, deed, register entry or published document.
  • Every parcel carries a confidence grade, so uncertainty is visible rather than hidden.
  • The method, code and list of entities are published, so anyone can re-run or challenge the work.
  • The map states facts and sources. Interpretation lives in separate, clearly labelled commentary.
  • Releases are versioned with a changelog, and anyone can submit a correction.

What counts as “the Church”

The Catholic Church is not one legal owner. Open Talamh defines the entities it tracks and publishes those definitions.

Class Typical legal holder Where names come from
Dioceses (26) and parishes Diocesan trusts, named clerical trustees Charities Regulator, diocesan directories
Religious congregations and orders Trustee bodies, companies limited by guarantee, named trustees Charities Regulator, CRO, AMRI membership
Schools Patron trustees, boards of management, congregational trusts (e.g. Edmund Rice Schools Trust) Department of Education school lists, patron bodies
Health and care Voluntary hospital companies, congregational holding companies HSE Section 38 list, CRO
Lay Catholic bodies Companies, trusts CRO, Charities Regulator

Each parcel is assigned one ownership tier:

  1. Legal title. A Catholic entity or trustee is the registered owner on a folio.
  2. Beneficial interest. Title is held by individuals or a company in trust for a Catholic entity.
  3. Control without title. For example, patronage of a school on State-owned land, or a long lease.
  4. Former holding. Sold, transferred to the State, or handed over under redress agreements since 2002.

Confidence grades

Grade Evidence Shown on the map as
A A Land Registry folio names a Catholic entity as registered owner Confirmed
B A folio names individuals or a company, and a second source confirms they hold in trust for a Catholic entity Confirmed via trustee
C Registry of Deeds evidence only Probable
D Use, signage or a heritage record suggests Church ownership; title not yet checked Unverified lead (not counted in totals)
E Held since 2002 but since sold or transferred Former holding

The first Louth map is built entirely from open data, so every point on it is currently a grade D lead.

How ownership is established

The ground truth is the Land Registry folio: a mapped parcel with a named registered owner. About 93% of the State’s land area is registered. A Names Index search returns every folio registered to one exact name in one county, so the unit of work is name × county, not parcel.

  1. Collect the legal name of every diocese, parish, congregation, trust and school body, with every variant (prefixes such as “Trustees of”, Irish-language forms, abbreviations, former names). Keep extending it until a full round of checking new folios produces no new names. The list is published with a version number.
  2. Every search is logged, including those that return nothing. Each folio returned is pulled for its owner, burdens and location.
  3. Every church, convent, school and institution is looked up on the free Land Registry map to find its folio. Any site whose owner isn’t on the name list reveals a missing name. The share of known sites already found in step 2 measures how complete the search is.
  4. For land with no folio, the Registry of Deeds is searched by townland and by grantor and grantee name. These results get a lower grade.
  5. using the A to E scale above.
  6. A random sample is double-checked by a second researcher, totals are benchmarked against published figures (such as the 2009 panel valuations and RTÉ’s 800-property Christian Brothers portfolio), and each major entity is sent its list of parcels for a right of reply before publication.

Known blind spots

  • Beneficial ownership registers for trusts and companies are not public, so trusts are only visible where a second source links trustee to beneficiary.
  • Folios show legal title, not leases, licences or covenants.
  • Trustee names change over time, so each folio is recorded with the date it was retrieved.

Data sources

Location data is plentiful and open, while ownership data is closed and paid. So the work of this project is to bridge the two data sources.

Source What it gives Shows ownership? Access
Land Registry folios (Tailte Éireann) Registered owner, burdens, parcel map Yes, the ground truth Paid per search and per folio; free map search for folio numbers
Registry of Deeds, Henrietta Street Deeds since 1708, by grantor and townland Clues In person, by appointment
OpenStreetMap Churches, convents, religious land, schools, graveyards No Free (ODbL)
National Inventory of Architectural Heritage Historic churches, convents, presbyteries and schools, with original and current use No Free
Department of Education school lists (primary, post-primary) Every school with its ethos and location No (the patron is not always the landowner) Free
Valuation list (Tailte Éireann) Rateable properties with use, area and location No; places of worship are often exempt Free

Companies Registration Office Companies, directors, former names Names only Search free; documents paid
Ryan, McAleese and Mother and Baby Homes reports; Industrial Memories Institutions, operators, dates No Free
Townland and county boundaries Statutory boundaries No Free (CC BY)
Residential Property Price Register Sale prices since 2010 No Free
INSPIRE cadastral parcels (Tailte Éireann) Parcel shapes without owners No Licence to be confirmed

Publishing and licensing

Data is published under an open licence (CC BY 4.0, with layers derived from OpenStreetMap under ODbL), alongside the code, the entity list and a changelog. Folio boundary maps are subject to Tailte Éireann copyright, so until republication is agreed, parcels are published as points with folio numbers, which anyone can look up. Open Talamh maps organisations, not private individuals, and gives every major entity a right of reply before publication.
